A machine learning project that fails to reach its intended evaluation usually
reports one verdict, and the verdict hides which of several unrelated obstacles
actually stopped it. This study takes one chest-radiograph report-generation
pilot that did not reach its intended benchmark and separates the obstacles into
three kinds --- administrative, statistical and instrumental --- each checkable
on its own and each removable by a different party.
The first is administrative. The intended evaluation is a contact-gated
benchmark, and the project's own record lists \NLocks{} obstacles: \NPermissionLocks{}
permissions obtainable only by a person or an institution, \NArtifactLocks{}
evaluation file that is absent locally and arrives only with one of those
permissions, and \NSubstitutionLocks{} refusals to score a locally held corpus
under the benchmark's name. None is removed by
computation, and the record was written before hardware was rented rather than
after. The second is statistical and has two faces that are routinely merged.
One belongs to the corpus: of \NFindings{} labelled observations,
\NExceedSplit{} would need more reports than the entire frozen test split of
\NTest{} before a draw is expected to contain \MinPresent{} positives, so they
are unanswerable at this corpus size however much of it is labelled. The other
belongs to the draw: the pre-registered subsample of \NCohort{} reports,
selected by a salted hash fixed in advance, is not representative of the split
it came from, holding \SkewObserved{} positives for \SkewFinding{} against
\SkewExpected{} expected, an exact two-sided hypergeometric probability of
\SkewP{} against a Bonferroni threshold of \Bonferroni{}. Only \NScored{} of
\NFindings{} observations clear the pilot's presence gate, and at the expected
draw only \NScoredExpected{} would have, so the pilot's scorable surface is
itself a product of sampling variation. The third obstacle is instrumental, and
it appears on exactly those \NScored{} cells, where both controls fail. A
constant majority report misses every gold positive, and an indication-retrieval
control misses \RetMissOne{} and \RetMissTwo{} of them with one-sided \(90\%\)
upper limits of \RetUpperOne{} and \RetUpperTwo{}. The majority failure has a
specific and transferable cause: the constant report reads as an unremarkable
study, and the automatic labeller nonetheless marks it positive for
\MajAsserted{} on all \NCohort{} rows and never marks it as the observation
denoting nothing to report, so the trivial baseline is not the trivial floor it
is assumed to be. Scored on surface text over the full split instead, the same
two controls reach \SurfaceRetrievalF{}
and \SurfaceMajorityF{} token F1 and convey none of this. No benchmark result is
synthesised, no score is reported under the blocked benchmark's name, and every
pilot quantity is reported with the power label it was recorded with rather than
promoted past it.
